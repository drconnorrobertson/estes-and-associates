What to Do If You Get an IRS Audit Letter

Getting an IRS audit notice can be one of the most stressful moments for a taxpayer. Your first reaction might be panic, but here's the truth: IRS audits are manageable when you know what to do. The IRS conducts hundreds of thousands of audits every year, and most result in little to no additional tax owed when properly handled.

Step 1: Don't Panic, Act Now

The first thing to understand is that you have rights and deadlines. The IRS audit notice will specify what they want to examine and when you need to respond. Missing deadlines can cost you significantly, so immediate action is essential.

Step 2: Understand the Type of Audit

There are three main types of IRS audits:

  • Correspondence Audit: The IRS sends you a letter requesting specific documents by mail. These are usually the simplest audits involving one or two issues. You respond by mail.
  • Office Audit: The IRS asks you to meet at their office with specific documents. These are more involved than correspondence audits and require preparing and presenting your documentation in person.
  • Field Audit: An IRS auditor comes to your home or business for an extensive examination. Field audits are typically the most involved and require careful preparation.

Step 3: Gather Documentation Immediately

The IRS notice will specify which items they want to examine. Immediately gather all documentation supporting those items:

  • Bank statements and cancelled checks
  • Receipts and invoices
  • Credit card statements
  • Loan documents and statements
  • Depreciation schedules
  • Correspondence with vendors or customers

Step 4: Get Professional Representation

This is critical. The IRS expects professional representation. When you show up unprepared and represent yourself, it signals weakness. A tax professional knows how to:

  • Present documentation professionally
  • Explain why your positions are correct
  • Negotiate with auditors
  • Protect your rights throughout the process

Step 5: Respond to the IRS

The deadline in the IRS notice is firm. For correspondence audits, you'll respond by mail with your documentation. For office or field audits, you'll meet with the auditor at the specified location and date.

Never miss an IRS deadline. If you need more time, your representative can request an extension.

What Happens After Your Response

The auditor will review your documentation and either:

  • Accept your position: No additional tax owed. Your audit is closed.
  • Propose adjustments: The auditor believes additional tax is owed and will propose specific adjustments.
  • Request more information: The auditor may want additional documentation or explanations.

If the IRS Proposes Adjustments

If the auditor proposes adjustments (additional tax owed), you have rights:

  • Agree: You can agree with the adjustment and pay
  • Disagree: You can disagree and your representative can negotiate
  • Request Appeals Conference: If you disagree, you can request a conference with the IRS Appeals Division
  • Go to Tax Court: You can dispute the assessment in Tax Court

Key Takeaways

If you receive an IRS audit notice:

  • Don't panic. Audits are manageable with proper representation.
  • Act immediately. Deadlines are firm.
  • Get a tax professional. Professional representation dramatically improves outcomes.
  • Gather documentation. Being prepared shows you take the audit seriously.
  • Understand your rights. You can disagree with the IRS and appeal their decisions.
