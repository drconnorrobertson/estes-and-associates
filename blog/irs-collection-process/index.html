Detailed Timeline of Collection Actions

Understanding the specific timeline helps you understand your options and when to act. Here is the detailed progression of IRS collection:

Days 1-10: Initial Notice

The IRS mails you a Notice and Demand for Payment. This is your first formal notification. You have 10 days from receipt to pay. This is your last chance to resolve the debt before the IRS begins collection procedures.

Days 11-30: Grace Period

Even if you dont pay within 10 days, the IRS typically doesnt immediately levy or seize assets. They may send additional notices during this period. If you act now, you can still avoid collection.

Day 31+: Federal Tax Lien Filed

After 30 days, the IRS files a Federal Tax Lien. This is a public record that appears on your credit report and attaches to all your property. You cannot refinance your home, apply for new credit, or sell property without dealing with the lien.

How Levies Actually Work in Practice

A levy is a legal seizure of your property. It is important to understand how levies work in practice:

Bank Account Levy Process: The IRS sends your bank a levy notice. Your bank has 21 days to identify and freeze funds. After 21 days, your bank surrenders the funds to the IRS. During this 21-day period, you can appeal the levy or make arrangements to satisfy the debt.

Wage Garnishment Process: The IRS sends your employer a notice to garnish your wages. Your employer must comply within three days. The garnishment continues until you satisfy the debt or make other arrangements with the IRS. This can significantly impact your ability to pay living expenses.

Property Seizure Process: The IRS must provide 10 days notice before seizing property. During this time, you can appeal or negotiate. If no resolution is reached, the IRS will seize property at your home or business. The property is sold at public auction, with proceeds going to your tax debt.

What You Can Do After Collection Has Started

If collection has already started, you still have options to protect yourself:

  • Request levy release: If a levy is causing undue hardship, you can request the IRS release it while you make other arrangements.
  • Request Currently Not Collectible status: This pauses collection while you address financial hardship.
  • File a Collection Due Process hearing request within 30 days of the lien notice.
  • Negotiate installment agreement: Even in collection, you can often set up a payment plan to resolve your debt.
  • File an Offer in Compromise: Settle for less than you owe if you qualify based on your financial situation.

The State Role in Collection

If you owe state taxes in Missouri as well as federal taxes, Missouri has parallel collection procedures. Like the IRS, Missouri files tax liens, garnishes wages, and seizes property. You may face collection from both the federal government and the state simultaneously, which requires coordinated resolution.
