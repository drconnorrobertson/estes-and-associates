Missouri Withholding Tax Guide for Employees and Employers

Complete guide to Missouri income tax withholding requirements for employers and employees

April 30, 2026 Tax & Accounting

Understanding Missouri Withholding Tax

Missouri withholding tax is required from most employee paychecks. Both employers and employees need to understand withholding requirements to ensure proper tax compliance and avoid penalties. This guide covers everything you need to know about Missouri income tax withholding.

What is Missouri Withholding Tax?

Missouri withholding tax is the amount of state income tax that employers deduct from employee paychecks. Employers must withhold and remit this tax to the Missouri Department of Revenue on behalf of their employees. The amount withheld is based on the employee's W-4 form and actual earnings.

Who Must Withhold Missouri Tax?

Most employers doing business in Missouri with employees working in the state must withhold Missouri income tax. This applies to corporations, partnerships, sole proprietors, and other business entities. Federal contractors and certain organizations may have different requirements.

Missouri Tax Withholding Rates

Missouri uses a progressive tax system with tax rates ranging from 1.5 percent to 5.75 percent depending on income level. The amount withheld from each paycheck depends on the employee's gross pay, filing status, and W-4 withholding elections.

Form MO-W-4

The Missouri Form MO-W-4 (or federal Form W-4 with Missouri supplement) tells employers how much to withhold. Employees complete this form when hired and can update it when their circumstances change. The withholding allowances and other elections on this form determine the amount withheld from each paycheck.

Withholding Requirements for Employers

Employers must withhold Missouri tax based on the employee's Form MO-W-4 or federal W-4. Withholding must be accurate and timely. Employers must deposit withheld taxes monthly, quarterly, or in some cases more frequently, depending on the amount being withheld.

Quarterly Filings and Annual Reconciliation

Employers file quarterly withholding reports with Missouri Department of Revenue reporting taxes withheld. At year-end, employers issue Form MO-W-2 to employees showing taxes withheld during the year. This information helps employees determine if additional taxes are owed or refunds are due.

Penalties for Non-Compliance

Employers who fail to withhold proper Missouri tax, file required forms, or deposit taxes on time face penalties. These can include percentage penalties on underpayment amounts plus interest. Working with a professional ensures compliance and helps avoid costly penalties.

Special Situations

Certain situations affect withholding requirements. These include temporary employees, non-resident employees, independent contractors, and employees receiving special compensation like bonuses or severance. Each situation has specific withholding requirements that must be properly calculated.
