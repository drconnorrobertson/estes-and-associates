Can You Settle with the IRS for Less? OIC Explained

An Offer in Compromise (OIC) is one of the most powerful tools available to taxpayers with IRS debt. It allows you to settle your tax debt for significantly less than you owe. Yet most people don't know it exists or think they don't qualify.

The IRS accepts thousands of OICs every year. If your circumstances are right, this could be your path to financial relief from crushing tax debt.

What Is an Offer in Compromise?

An Offer in Compromise is an agreement with the IRS to settle your tax debt for an amount less than you owe. Instead of paying your full tax bill, you make a lump sum payment (or series of payments) and the remaining debt is forgiven.

Who Qualifies for an OIC?

The IRS will consider an OIC if one of three things is true:

  • Doubt as to Correctness: There's genuine doubt about whether the tax amount owed is correct
  • Doubt as to Liability: There's genuine doubt about your legal liability for the tax
  • Doubt as to Collectability: You lack the financial ability to pay your full tax debt, even over time

Most OICs are granted based on "doubt as to collectability," which means the IRS concludes you simply don't have the ability to pay your entire tax debt.

How Much Can You Save?

OIC settlements vary dramatically. We've negotiated settlements ranging from 10% of the tax owed to 90%, depending on the person's circumstances. The key is properly presenting your financial situation to the IRS.

Who Is a Good Candidate for an OIC?

OICs work well for people in these situations:

  • Self-Employed with Low Income: You have minimal income and few assets. An OIC settles the debt for what you can actually afford.
  • Recently Unemployed: You're facing unemployment or underemployment and can't sustain payment plans.
  • Business Owner with Minimal Assets: Your business failed but you have limited personal assets to seize.
  • Approaching Retirement: You're near retirement with limited liquid assets.
  • Disabled or in Health Crisis: You have medical issues creating financial hardship.

The OIC Process: Step by Step

Here's what to expect:

  1. Financial Analysis: We conduct a detailed analysis of your income, assets, and living expenses
  2. Offer Calculation: Based on this analysis, we calculate an appropriate offer amount
  3. Form Preparation: We prepare Form 656 (Offer in Compromise) and gather all required supporting documentation
  4. Submission: We submit your offer to the IRS along with your initial payment (typically 20% of the offer amount)
  5. IRS Review: The IRS reviews your offer and supporting documentation (typically 3-6 months)
  6. Negotiation: If the IRS counters with a higher amount, we negotiate on your behalf
  7. Acceptance: Once accepted, you pay the agreed amount and the debt is settled

What Happens While Your OIC is Pending?

One of the best aspects of filing an OIC is that the IRS typically suspends collection action while your offer is pending. This means:

  • No wage garnishment
  • No bank levies
  • No new liens

This breathing room gives you time to get your situation organized while we work on your case.

Why Use a Professional for Your OIC?

The OIC process is complex. Success depends on properly presenting your financial situation to convince the IRS that you lack the ability to pay. Here's why professionals matter:

  • We know exactly what financial information the IRS requires
  • We understand how the IRS evaluates offers
  • We know IRS expense standards and how to challenge them
  • We negotiate directly with the IRS
  • Professional representation dramatically increases acceptance rates

Common OIC Misconceptions

Myth: "OICs are only for people with zero income."

Reality: OICs are granted to people with regular income when they can't afford to pay their tax debt and sustain living expenses.

Myth: "The IRS almost never accepts OICs."

Reality: The IRS accepts thousands of OICs every year. The key is proper presentation.

Myth: "I can do an OIC myself."

Reality: While possible, professional representation increases acceptance rates significantly.
