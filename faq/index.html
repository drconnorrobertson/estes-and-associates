Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common questions about our services.

Tax Preparation & Filing

When should I file my tax return?

Individual federal tax returns are due April 15th each year. We recommend filing as early as possible to receive refunds faster and resolve any issues quickly. We can handle filing deadlines for businesses, estates, and other entity types.

What if I can't file by April 15th?

You can request an extension, which gives you until October 15th to file your return. However, an extension to file is not an extension to pay. Estimated taxes should still be paid by April 15th to avoid penalties.

What documents do I need for tax preparation?

Typically, you'll need your prior year return, all W-2s and 1099s, mortgage interest statements, records of charitable donations, education expenses, business expenses, and documentation of any major life changes. We'll provide a checklist.

Can I deduct my home office?

Yes, if you use part of your home exclusively for business, you can deduct home office expenses. There are two methods: simplified ($5 per square foot, max 300 sq ft) or actual expenses. We can determine which method saves you more.

Accounting & Bookkeeping

Do I really need a bookkeeper?

If your business has employees or significant income, professional bookkeeping is essential. Accurate books help you understand your profitability, are required for taxes, and are needed if you apply for loans. The time and money saved far outweighs the cost.

How often should I get financial statements?

Many business owners benefit from monthly financial statements so they can make informed decisions. Minimum would be quarterly. We can prepare them on whatever schedule works for you.

IRS Problems

What if I owe the IRS?

You have several options. You can pay in full, set up an installment agreement (payment plan), request an Offer in Compromise (settle for less), or request Currently Not Collectible status (temporary delay). We'll help you understand which option is best.

Can I settle my IRS debt for less?

Maybe. An Offer in Compromise allows you to settle for less if you can prove you lack the ability to pay the full amount. Not everyone qualifies, but many people do. We can analyze your situation.

What if the IRS garnished my wages?

We can often stop wage garnishment quickly by placing your account in Currently Not Collectible status, negotiating an installment agreement, or pursuing other relief options. Contact us immediately as we may be able to help quickly.

What is an IRS audit?

An audit is an IRS examination of your tax return to verify accuracy. Most audits are correspondence audits (handled by mail). We represent you throughout the audit process and handle all IRS communication.

What if I haven't filed taxes in years?

Getting current is important and doable. We help you file back returns, negotiate with the IRS, and often minimize penalties. The longer you wait, the worse it gets, so contact us now.
