IRS Audit Defense: Let Us Represent You

Getting an IRS audit notice is stressful. Your first reaction might be panic, but here's the truth: IRS audits are manageable when you're properly represented. The IRS conducts hundreds of thousands of audits every year, and most result in little or no additional tax owed when properly defended.

At Estes & Associates, we represent clients throughout the audit process. We gather documentation, respond to IRS requests, and present your case professionally so you don't have to.

Types of IRS Audits

Correspondence Audit

The IRS sends you a notice requesting specific documentation by mail. These are usually the simplest audits involving one or two issues.

Office Audit

The IRS asks you to meet at their office with specific documents. These are more involved than correspondence audits.

Field Audit

The IRS auditor comes to your home or business for an extensive examination. Field audits are typically the most involved.

Audit Issues We Handle

We defend clients on virtually all audit issues including:

  • Business deductions and depreciation
  • Home office deductions
  • Vehicle expenses
  • Travel and entertainment expenses
  • Charitable contributions
  • Rental property deductions
  • Capital gains and losses
  • Self-employment income
  • Investment income reporting

Our Audit Defense Process

Here's how we handle your audit:

  1. Initial Review: We review the audit notice and understand what the IRS is questioning
  2. Documentation Gathering: We gather all documentation supporting your tax positions (receipts, invoices, bank statements, etc.)
  3. Response Preparation: We prepare a thorough, professional response to IRS requests
  4. Communication: We communicate directly with the IRS auditor on your behalf
  5. Negotiation: If the IRS proposes adjustments, we negotiate to minimize additional tax owed
  6. Resolution: We explain the audit results and help you understand your next steps and appeal rights if needed

Why Representation Matters

Representing yourself in an audit puts you at a disadvantage:

  • IRS Auditors Expect Professionals: Auditors regularly deal with tax professionals. When you show up unprepared, it signals weakness
  • Documentation Presentation: We know how to organize and present documentation to support your position
  • Technical Knowledge: We understand tax law and can explain why your position is correct
  • Negotiation Skills: We negotiate with auditors routinely and know when to compromise and when to stand firm
  • Emotional Distance: Audits are stressful. We stay objective and professional while you stay informed

What Happens After an Audit?

If the IRS proposes adjustments, you have rights:

  • You can agree with the adjustments
  • You can request an Appeals conference with the IRS Appeals division
  • You can go to Tax Court if you disagree with the assessment

We'll help you understand your options and choose the best path forward.
