Innocent Spouse Relief: Escape Liability for Your Spouse's Tax Debt

You filed jointly with your spouse, but now the IRS says you owe taxes you didn't know about, didn't create, and don't believe are your responsibility. Can the IRS really hold you liable for your spouse's taxes? Yes, they can. Joint return liability means both spouses are fully liable for the entire tax debt.

But here's the hope: Innocent Spouse Relief allows you to get relief from joint liability if your spouse created the tax problem and you can meet certain requirements. This is a powerful protection for taxpayers in your situation.

Types of Spouse Relief

Innocent Spouse Relief (IRC 6015(b))

You can get relief from your joint liability if you can prove you didn't know about and had no reason to know about an understatement of tax created by your spouse's omission or misreporting of income, deductions, or credits.

Separate Liability Relief (IRC 6015(c))

If you're no longer married or are legally separated or divorced, you may qualify for separate liability relief for a jointly filed return. This relief allocates the tax between you and your spouse based on who created the understatement.

Equitable Relief (IRC 6015(f))

If you don't qualify for relief under the above sections, you may qualify for equitable relief based on the facts and circumstances of your situation.

Requirements for Innocent Spouse Relief

To qualify for innocent spouse relief, you generally must prove:

  • A substantial understatement of tax was created by your spouse (omitted income, improper deductions, etc.)
  • You didn't know and had no reason to know about the understatement
  • Based on all facts and circumstances, it would be unfair to hold you liable
  • You did not transfer assets to your spouse as part of a plan to avoid tax

Common Situations Where Relief Applies

  • Hidden Affair Income: Your spouse had unreported business or employment income you didn't know about
  • Investment Income Omission: Spouse received income from investments you didn't know they had
  • Business Deductions: Spouse claimed false business deductions for a business you didn't know about or wasn't involved with
  • Spouse Prepared Return: Your spouse prepared the return alone and didn't show it to you
  • Marital Problems: You were separated or experiencing marital problems when the return was filed
  • Spouse Controls Finances: Your spouse controlled all finances and kept you in the dark

Situations Where Relief Doesn't Apply

Relief is less likely if:

  • You were involved in the tax underpayment or signed the return knowing it was false
  • The income or deductions appeared on the return and you should have known about them
  • You had full access to financial records and actively chose not to look
  • You benefited from the underpayment

The Relief Process

Here's how innocent spouse relief works:

  1. Assessment: We determine whether your situation qualifies for innocent spouse relief and which type
  2. Documentation: We gather documentation supporting your claim of innocence
  3. Form Preparation: We prepare Form 8857 (Innocent Spouse Relief Request) with detailed explanation
  4. Submission: We submit the form to the IRS within the required deadline (typically 2 years)
  5. IRS Review: The IRS reviews your claim and issues a determination
  6. Appeal: If the IRS denies relief, we can appeal to the Tax Court

Timeline Matters

There's typically a 2-year statute of limitations for requesting innocent spouse relief. If you're concerned about a tax situation with your spouse, don't delay in getting professional help. Missing the deadline can cost you your relief option.
