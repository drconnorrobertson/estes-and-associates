Offers in Compromise: Settle Your IRS Debt

An Offer in Compromise (OIC) is an agreement with the IRS to settle your tax debt for less than you owe. It's one of the most powerful tools available to taxpayers with tax problems, yet most people don't know it exists or think they don't qualify.

The IRS accepts thousands of OICs each year. If your circumstances are right, we can help you get relief from your tax debt through an OIC.

Who Qualifies for an Offer in Compromise?

The IRS will consider an OIC if:

  • There's genuine doubt about the correctness of the tax amount owed, OR
  • There's genuine doubt about your ability to pay the full amount, OR
  • Paying the full amount would create economic hardship (doubt as to collectability)

Most OICs are granted based on doubt as to collectability, which means the IRS concludes you don't have the financial means to pay your entire tax debt.

How an Offer in Compromise Works

Here's the basic process:

  1. Financial Analysis: We analyze your income, assets, and expenses to determine what you can reasonably pay
  2. Offer Amount Calculation: Based on this analysis, we calculate an appropriate offer amount
  3. Documentation Preparation: We prepare Form 656 (Offer in Compromise) and supporting financial documentation
  4. Submission: We submit your offer to the IRS along with your initial payment
  5. IRS Review: The IRS reviews your offer and supporting documentation
  6. Negotiation: We negotiate with the IRS if they counter with a different amount
  7. Acceptance: Once accepted, you pay the agreed amount and the debt is settled

What Happens During the OIC Process?

While your OIC is pending, the IRS typically suspends collection action. This means no wage garnishment, no bank levies, and no new liens. This gives you breathing room while we work on your case.

How Much Can You Save?

OIC settlements vary dramatically depending on your situation. We've negotiated settlements ranging from 10% of the tax owed to 90%, depending on circumstances. The key is properly presenting your financial situation to the IRS.

Common OIC Scenarios

Here are some situations where OICs work well:

  • Self-Employed with Low Income: You have minimal income and few assets. An OIC settles the debt for what you can afford
  • Recently Unemployed: You're facing unemployment or underemployment and can't sustain payment plans
  • Business Owner with Minimal Personal Assets: Your business failed but you have limited personal assets
  • Approaching Retirement: You're near retirement with limited liquid assets
