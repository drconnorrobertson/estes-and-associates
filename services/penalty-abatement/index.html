IRS Penalty Abatement: Reduce What You Owe

The IRS doesn't just charge you taxes. They charge penalties on top of the taxes owed, which can sometimes equal the taxes themselves. Common penalties include failure-to-file penalties (5% per month), failure-to-pay penalties, accuracy-related penalties, and more.

But here's the good news: the IRS can and does abate (forgive) penalties when circumstances justify relief. If you have reasonable cause, the IRS may eliminate penalties entirely, saving you thousands of dollars.

Common IRS Penalties

Failure-to-File Penalty

5% of unpaid taxes per month you're late filing (max 25%). This penalty can be substantial when you owe a significant tax bill and file late.

Failure-to-Pay Penalty

0.5% of unpaid taxes per month (max 25%). Added if you file on time but don't pay the full amount.

Accuracy-Related Penalty

20% of underpayment due to negligence, substantial understatement, or improper valuation.

Fraud Penalty

75% of underpayment due to tax fraud. This is serious and requires aggressive defense.

Estimated Tax Penalties

Penalties for not making adequate estimated tax payments throughout the year.

Reasonable Cause for Penalty Abatement

The IRS will abate penalties if you show "reasonable cause." What constitutes reasonable cause?

  • First-Time Penalty Abatement: If this is your first penalty, the IRS may abate it automatically (First Penalty Abatement or FPA)
  • Circumstances Beyond Your Control: Illness, injury, fire, flood, or other events that prevented timely filing or payment
  • Reliance on Professional Advice: You reasonably relied on a tax professional's incorrect advice
  • Business Circumstances: Accounting system failures, recent business startup, unexpected income sources
  • Good Compliance History: Years of timely filing and payment demonstrate the error was unusual

How Much Can You Save?

Penalty abatement amounts vary tremendously. A single taxpayer with a $5,000 tax bill facing a $1,000 failure-to-file penalty could save the entire penalty. A business facing $10,000+ in penalties could potentially save most or all of those penalties with proper reasonable cause documentation.

The Key: Proper Documentation

Successful penalty abatement depends on presenting a compelling reasonable cause argument supported by documentation. We help by:

  • Analyzing your situation to identify valid reasonable cause arguments
  • Gathering documentation that supports your reasonable cause claim
  • Preparing a professional written statement for the IRS
  • Submitting the abatement request with maximum persuasiveness
  • Following up with the IRS to ensure your request is considered

When to Request Abatement

You don't have to wait until you're under audit to request penalty abatement. In many cases, we proactively request abatement when:

  • You're filing a late return (we can request FPA and potentially First Penalty Abatement)
  • You're resolving back taxes through an installment agreement
  • You're negotiating an Offer in Compromise
  • You're responding to an IRS notice

Don't Leave Money on the Table

If the IRS has assessed penalties on your account, don't automatically pay them. Many penalties can be abated with the right approach. Let us review your situation and determine if penalty abatement makes sense for you.
